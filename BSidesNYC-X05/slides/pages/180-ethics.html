Uses and Ethics

  • Checking safety of 3rd-party code that is embedded with your code.
  • Understanding crashes, and code written by others who write in an opaque style.
  • Understanding security. Writing obfuscation tools and encryption methods.
  • I do not approve of using decompilation or bytecode patching for illegal activity.
    Don't do it. Even worse, is soliciting my help in such endeavors.

    There are valid uses for reverse engineering bytecode. My initial interest was point two: in getting more fine-grained detail information of code that I and others wrote.

    A CTO once approached me. He was worried about using a Google Python library that had to be embedded in his company's proproetary code. He wanted to be sure that this library code that didn't come with source code, didn't look at the company's proprietary code.

    Understanding what is possible also makes it possible to make things harder for disassembly and decompilation, and what methods are more secure.

    Finally, and most important: I do not approve of using decompilation and bytecode patching for illegal activity.

    Don't do it. Even worse, is soliciting my help in such endeavors.
